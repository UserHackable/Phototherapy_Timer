20.    TROUBLESHOOTING
Generally, these devices are very reliable; however, there may be occasion in the lifetime of a device
when a fuse blows; or a bulb, ballast or timer fails.

If this happens, first remember the possibility of the person working on the system getting
burned, even with exposure times as short as 10 seconds! Supply the troubleshooter with this
User's Manual. Wear ultraviolet protective eyewear and clothes that will block the light. Wear a
sunscreen with a minimum sun protection factor of 40 (SPF40) on any exposed skin and minimize
exposure, but do not get any sunscreen on the bulbs or Clear Acrylic Window. When testing, consider
temporarily covering the system with a blanket to block the light.

            DANGER – Ultraviolet radiation. Protect exposed skin when testing and minimize
            exposure time. Overexposure can cause severe burning. Wear protective eyewear.
            Failure to may result in severe burns or long-term injury to the eyes.


Electrical work should be performed by qualified individuals. Detach the power supply cord and
remove the key before removing any bulbs or the back cover.


           DANGER – Disconnect the power supply cord and remove the key when cleaning or
           changing the bulbs, or when servicing the system.


No Device Power

If the MASTER device and its timer do not light up:
     1. Verify that the electrical wall outlet has power by plugging in some other appliance. If there is
        no power, check the circuit breaker in the building's electrical panel and reset it if necessary.
     2. Check that the device's power supply cord is fully inserted into the wall outlet.
     3. Check that the power supply cord is pushed fully down into the power inlet on the top of the
        MASTER device.
     4. Ensure that the switchlock key is ON.
     5. Consider that the system may not work with a ground fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) outlet, or
        that the GFCI outlet is defective. Try a different outlet.

If the problem persists, it is possible that a device fuse has blown. Detach the power supply cord and
remove the key, then on the top of the MASTER device, unscrew the top of the black plastic
fuseholders (or open the fuseholder drawer) and remove the fuse(s). Inspect the fuse(s) and replace
as necessary. Always use a fuse of the same type and amp rating, as listed on the labeling. Never
use a fuse with a larger amp rating. Note that 120-volt devices have only one (1) active fuse,
whereas 208-240 volt devices have two (2) active fuses.

Broken Bulb(s)

If a bulb breaks, for clean-up instructions see "What to Do if a Fluorescent Light Bulb Breaks in Your
Home" in Appendix B.

Some Bulbs Do Not Turn On

First, before proceeding below, if one or more entire devices do not turn on, check that the device
connection cables between the devices are properly connected as described in the ADD-ON DEVICE
INSTALLATION section.

The bulbs are electrically grouped in pairs to a "ballast". For 2-bulb "E720" devices, there is only one
ballast. For 4-bulb "E740" devices there are two ballasts, with the two outermost bulbs wired to one
ballast, and the two innermost bulbs wired to the other ballast. For 6-bulb "E760" devices, a third
ballast is wired to the “middle bulbs” (in bulb positions 2 and 5 counting left to right); and so on. The
bulbs are always electrically grouped in pairs in a symmetrical pattern to the vertical centerline of the
device.

For a group of two bulbs wired to the same ballast to operate properly, both bulbs must be functional.
That is, one bad bulb or lampholder connection will cause the other bulb to also not function. So, if a
group of two bulbs wired to the same ballast does not turn on, there are two likely possibilities:
a) one of the two bulbs is defective, or not installed correctly in the sockets,
b) the ballast is defective.

To determine which, if available, swap two known working bulbs with the two non-working bulbs and
retest. Refer to the Bulb Replacement section of this manual for instructions on bulb removal as
necessary. If the problem follows the bulbs, then they are the problem source and one at a time put a
non-working bulb back in to pinpoint which bulb has failed. Check the condition of the end pins and
the sockets and retest. If the problem stays with the ballast, then it is defective and will need
replacement.

If known working bulbs are not available (for example if you have only a single 2-bulb MASTER
device), the best option may be to speculate and change the ballast, because a ballast is less
expensive than bulbs and is easier to ship. Contact Solarc for ballast replacement parts and
procedures.

Timer Failure

If a timer fails or behaves erratically, please contact Solarc for further instructions and do not use the
system.

APPENDIX D
EXPOSURE GUIDELINE TABLES "EGTs"                               (TREATMENT TIMES)
This appendix includes the following UVB-Narrowband Exposure Guideline Tables (EGTs). These
important tables will guide you through your initial and ongoing treatment times. For each EGT group
there is a specific page for psoriasis, vitiligo and eczema.

Do the following:
1. Carefully select the EGT group for the SYSTEM you have, be it a single panel or one of the
    multidirectional (MD) arrangements.
2. Select the skin condition to be treated, and in the case of psoriasis your Skin Type.
3. To avoid mix-ups, with a pencil cross off any tables not used.
4. Consider moving or copying your specific table to the front of this User's Manual for quick
    reference.

    Take great care to select the correct table! Please contact Solarc for help if necessary.

SYSTEM▼        DESCRIPTION▼

E760M          6-bulb MASTER Single Panel
MD66           6-bulb MASTER plus one 6-bulb ADD-ON - Multidirectional with 12 bulbs total
MD666+         6-bulb MASTER plus two or more 6-bulb ADD-ONs - Multidirectional with 18 bulbs
               total
MD262          6-bulb MASTER with a 2-bulb ADD-ON on each side - Multidirectional with 10 bulbs
               total

Multidirectional (MD) systems, and in particular those consisting of three or more devices, are
effective for the simultaneous treatment of the front (or back) of the body and a portion of both sides,
so that typically only two treatment positions are required (front & back), and to reduce the patient's
total treatment time. The devices should ideally be angled so that they are all 8 to 12 inches from the
bulbs to the closest skin surface.

A multidirectional (MD) system provides its greatest irradiance (light power) in the lateral center of the
device assembly, with somewhat less irradiance moving outward towards the ADD-ON devices. To
equalize the amount of light to all skin areas, patients may consider rotating their body during
treatments. Alternatively, if some skin areas need more light than others, consider targeting those
areas on the MASTER device.

The treatment times listed are based on the system's maximum nominal steady state Irradiance as
listed in SPECIFICATIONS TABLE 2, located at the front of this User's Manual.

*** Vitiligo Treatment Time Discussion:
For vitiligo, the optimal dose is when the depigmented white skin area (the “lesion”) reaches a state of
very mild erythema, which is the slightest onset of sunburn. For example, 6 to 24 hours after a treatment,
the lesion should ideally reach a very light pink colour, but it should NOT be solid bright pink or red.
Depending on the individual, as the bulbs age and lose power, and as your skin builds tolerance; this could
mean that your treatment times have to be increased beyond the maximum listed above, but if so typically
only after many months of use. Whatever the case, always remember, NEVER GET BURNED!
For significant vitiligo involvement, Solarc recommends full-body phototherapy with both healthy and
vitiligo-affected skin areas receiving the same dose, so there are both local effects (in the lesion) and
systemic effects (from the healthy skin, such as Vitamin D and other UVB induced biologic processes).
Consult your physician regarding the best strategy for long term maintenance and maximum
treatment time / dose limits.

** If your skin ever becomes more than slightly pink at any stage in the schedule, drop back to the
previous exposure time. If you become uncomfortably red or sunburned after treatment, skip the next one
or two exposures and drop back to the previous exposure time. If more than one treatment is missed in
succession, reduce the exposure time further. When in doubt, always use the lower exposure time.
Never get burned!
