12.       BODY POSITIONS


             WARNINGS – BODY POSITIONING


      •   The minimum recommended exposure distance is 8 to 12 inches (20-30cm) from the
          front of the bulbs to the nearest skin surface of the body. Decreasing the distance
          between your body and the bulbs increases the chance of overexposure. The exposure
          guideline tables are based on a distance of 10 inches (25cm).
      •   Be consistent with your treatment positions and method. Make changes gradually over
          many treatment sessions.
      •   Do not overlap treatment areas.
      •   Failure to observe these warnings may result in overexposure and skin burns.


A “Body Position” is the patient’s body position held in front of the system for one complete timer
countdown cycle “treatment time”. For each body position, the patient keeps their body generally
facing the system the same direction; that is, they do not spin around during the timer countdown
cycle. The number of different body positions needed for full 360˚ coverage is influenced by the
patient’s body size (torso diameter) and the number of devices. Only one body position is necessary
for full booths, but it may be beneficial to rotate during treatment to generalize the UV light delivery.

General guidelines for body positioning are:
1. The minimum recommended exposure distance is 8 to 12" (20-30cm) from the front of the bulbs
   to the nearest skin surface of the body. Decreasing the distance between your body and the bulbs
   increases the chance of overexposure. The exposure guideline tables are based on a distance of
   10" (25cm). Some patients find it useful to check their centering and distance by briefly touching
   the sides of the devices with their hands. For ease of measurement, 10" from the front of the
   bulbs is equivalent to 9-5/8” from the ~1/2” wide reflector center face.
2. When using more than one body position, do not significantly overlap skin areas, as this may
   cause local skin overexposure.
3. Typically the feet are placed 8 to 12" (20-30cm) apart on the floor to get the light to the inside of
4. the legs. However, if only one or two devices are used, this distance might be reduced to favor
   the outside of the legs.

5. Typically the arms are held to the side spaced 4 to 6" (10-15 cm) from the body with the palms of
   the hands facing back. However, several different arm positions could be used per the pictures
   that follow.
6. The maximum Irradiance is at the lateral center of the device(s). To reduce local overexposure,
   consider moving your body a bit during treatment to balance the UVB dose to various skin areas.
   For full booths, consider rotating your entire body periodically.
7. If some skin areas are particularly bad, target those areas with more ultraviolet light. This can be
   done by taking special body positions, for example to target the elbows as shown in picture 5
   below; or by using longer treatment times for certain body positions.
8. If the legs need more treatment than the upper part of the body, consider standing on a stool.
   Note that all fluorescent bulb tubes have greater intensity in the center portion of the bulb tube
   than at the ends.
9. To maximize the predictability of your treatment results, try to be consistent with your treatment
   method. Use the same sequence of body positions and try to keep the same distance from the
   bulbs. Make changes gradually over many treatment sessions to avoid overexposure.


UVB light has both local and systemic effects, so patients should consider treating more than just the
skin lesions. It is acceptable and likely preferable to also treat the healthy skin around the lesions and
elsewhere on the body, because every square inch of exposed skin contributes to the systemic
effects, resulting in better results and other health benefits. However, some skin areas should have
minimal UV exposure, such as the genitals (unless affected) and skin areas that have received
considerable natural sun exposure in the patient’s lifetime; such as the face, head, shoulders,
forearms and upper chest. These areas can be covered using a mask or clothing such as a thong,
but first check that the material adequately blocks light. Women should consider wearing a bra to
prevent local overexposure. For men, a cut-off sock (ideally black in colour) can be used to cover
both the penis and the scrotum. Alternatively, a sunscreen of adequate SPF factor could be used, but
that is messy, requires application of foreign material to the skin and may contaminate the Clear
Acrylic Window reducing UVB output. To protect the lips, consider applying UV-protective lip balm
before treatment, or pursing the lips together during treatment.

If the devices do NOT form a complete booth, the patient needs to take more than one body position
to provide 360˚ coverage all around the body. Here are some examples:
• if the devices provide 180˚ coverage, then two (2) body positions would be required, which
     typically would be: front-side, back-side;
• if the devices provide 90˚ coverage, then four (4) body positions would be required, which
     typically would be: front-side, left-side, back-side, right-side;
• if a small number of devices are used, the amount of coverage and number of body positions
     needs to be estimated.
If your goal is to evaluate the effectiveness of the system, you may wish to discuss with your
physician the possibility of treating only select areas of your body and comparing the result to areas
that were not treated. For example, you could treat your front side only, and compare the results to
your untreated back side. If using these methods, it is important to carefully maintain separate
treatment schedules for each treatment area. If you later decide to begin treating both areas, do not
suddenly introduce the untreated area to the same treatment times as the treated area. Instead, build
up treatment times from the beginning per the exposure guideline tables.

Use the Phototherapy Calendars located in Appendix C to record your treatment times and results,
and to help you determine your next treatment time according to the exposure guidelines. Bring your
treatment log to your physician visits. For years beyond those provided, use the generic Calendar (no
weekends or year identified) or go to SolarcSystems.com>Information.

One set of ultraviolet protective goggles is supplied with the MASTER device and must be used for
every treatment, regardless of the exposure time. Adjust the headband so that they seal with your
face and do not leak any light. Never look at the light without using the ultraviolet protective
goggles provided. The bulbs have what appears to be an innocent blue glow; however, what
you do not see is high levels of invisible ultraviolet radiation that can severely damage your
eyes. Use only eye protection approved for UVB such as those supplied with this SolRx Unit. Do not
use any other eyewear. If in doubt, check with your physician or contact Solarc Systems toll free at
866-813-3357 (705-739-8279). This applies to all persons exposed to the ultraviolet light, such as a
supervising parent or guardian. Note: If the goggles are covering your eyes, it is acceptable to look at
the light, but ideally only briefly.

         WARNING: Always wear the ultraviolet protective goggles supplied; failure to may
         result in severe burns or long-term injury to the eyes.
